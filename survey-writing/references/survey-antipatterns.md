# Survey Anti-Patterns

## Paper list with a decorative topic sentence

Symptom:

> Methods use several forms of supervision. A uses labels. B uses time. C uses interventions.

Why it fails:

The opening labels the list but does not synthesize it.

Fix:

Frame the paragraph around what each supervision source reveals and what assumption it replaces.

## Disguised citation dump

Symptom:

A paragraph ends with several unrelated applications and per-item citations.

Why it fails:

The sentence adds bibliography coverage but no analytical structure.

Fix:

Group only works that support one claim. Remove tangential works or move them to a table.

## Retrofitted taxonomy

Symptom:

Categories reproduce existing section names and need many exceptions.

Why it fails:

The taxonomy describes the draft rather than organizing the field.

Fix:

Return to the synthesis matrix. Choose axes based on assumptions, goals, or technical consequences.

## Forced miscellaneous category

Symptom:

“Other approaches” contains works with no shared property.

Why it fails:

The category hides taxonomic weakness.

Fix:

Add a defensible axis, allow multi-label placement, or discuss the works briefly without a shared label.

## Template paragraph

Symptom:

Every paragraph defines a category, describes three papers, and ends with “Specifically.”

Why it fails:

Uniform structure makes synthesis feel mechanical and paper-centered.

Fix:

Vary the analytical relation. Use contrast, progression, shared limitation, or evaluation disagreement.

## Repeated opening and closing

Symptom:

The closing restates the branch's advantage from the opening.

Why it fails:

The section consumes space without advancing the analysis.

Fix:

Use the opening to define the branch and the closing to state its limitation, unresolved question, or connection to the next branch.

## Selective numerical promotion

Symptom:

Two favored papers receive benchmark numbers while neighboring methods receive qualitative descriptions.

Why it fails:

The selection appears biased and may compare incompatible settings.

Fix:

Use a systematic comparison table or remove isolated numbers from prose.

## Empty future direction

Symptom:

> Future work should improve scalability and robustness.

Why it fails:

It names aspirations without a research problem.

Fix:

Tie the direction to a documented limit, identify the obstacle, and suggest what evidence would demonstrate progress.

## Incomplete cross-category claim

Symptom:

A paragraph promises to compare the taxonomy but discusses only the easiest categories.

Why it fails:

The reader cannot tell whether omitted categories contradict the finding.

Fix:

Cover every relevant category or narrow the claim explicitly.

## AI-like redundancy

Symptom:

Adjacent sentences repeat a claim using “Moreover,” “Furthermore,” or a new adjective.

Why it fails:

The paragraph sounds generated and wastes space.

Fix:

Delete any sentence whose removal loses no fact, comparison, or judgment. Use logical transitions that name the actual relation.
